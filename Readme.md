# Host Rock Comparison for a High-Level Waste Repository

**Comparing rock salt, claystone and crystalline rock as host rocks for a deep geological repository – with a focus on repository design and operational safety.**

> 🚧 Work in progress – comparison table complete, brief in preparation

## Guiding question

What are each host rock's strengths and weaknesses, and how do they change the repository design and its operational safety?

## Background

Germany is searching for a site for a repository for high-level radioactive waste. Three host rocks are being considered: rock salt, claystone and crystalline rock. Each one leads to a different repository concept, a different barrier system and different risks during operation.

Most host rock comparisons focus on long-term safety. This project also looks at the **operating phase**: excavation, heat and ventilation, water inflow, emplacement and retrievability.

## Comparison at a glance

| Criterion | Rock salt | Claystone | Crystalline rock |
|---|---|---|---|
| Permeability (intact, in situ) | < 10⁻²⁰ m² | < 10⁻¹⁹ m² | < 10⁻¹⁸ m² (unfractured) |
| Design temperature limit | 200 °C | 100 °C | 100 °C |
| Mechanical behaviour | viscous (creep) | plastic to brittle | brittle |
| Excavation stability | self-supporting | support necessary | high (unfractured) to low (fractured) |
| Radionuclide sorption | very low | very high | medium to high |
| Main containment barrier | geological barrier | geological barrier | engineered barriers (canister + bentonite) |
